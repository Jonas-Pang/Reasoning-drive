#### Task
From the pages below, list every passage that gives the meaning of a marking on the road surface for a driver in
a lane. One passage = one rule, usually one or two sentences. If the pages hold no such passage, return an empty list.

#### What counts
- lines along the road: lane lines, centre lines, edge lines, broken, solid, double, solid-and-broken, dotted lines;
  lines that guide, merge or separate lanes; lane-change or no-overtaking lines
- markings inside a lane: arrows, words (ONLY, BUS, STOP and the like), symbols, waiting areas, two-way left-turn lanes,
  reserved, bus, HOV, tidal or reversible lanes
- lines across a lane: stop lines, yield lines, crosswalks; marked areas: hatched or chevron areas, no-stopping grids

#### What does not count
Sizes, colours of paint mixtures, materials, reflectivity, spacing tables, figures, legal procedure, signs that are not
painted on the road, and sentences that only say where a marking is installed without saying what it means for a driver.

#### How to copy
- text_original: the passage in the language of the standard, word for word from the pages. Leave out figure and table
  references. Keep it short: only the sentences that carry the rule.
- text_en: the same passage in English (a translation if the standard is not in English; then text_en_is_translation is true).
- section: the clause number as printed (for example 4.2.2 a or 3B.06 05); page: the page number shown in the page marker.
- marking_type: a short English name of the marking (for example "white solid lane line", "left-turn arrow").
- element: boundary (a line beside the lane), lane_marking (inside the lane), lane_type (the lane as a whole),
  transverse (across the lane), area (a marked area) or general (lines in general).
- norm: the force of the passage as the standard words it: mandatory (shall, must, shall not, may not, is prohibited, and
  the same words in the standard's language, such as 应, 不得, 禁止, しなければならない), recommended (should, 宜),
  permissive (may, 可, ことができる) or informative (a definition, description or purpose without such a word).
- actions: what the passage itself says about each movement: allowed, prohibited or not_stated. Use prohibited also for
  "shall not", "must not", "is not permitted" and for restrictions; use not_stated when the passage does not speak to it.
