How to fill in the answer:
- description: step 1 in one sentence, visible features only.
- evidence: "full" if every feature that decides the behaviour is visible (the colour and pattern of a line: at least one dash and one gap for a broken line, an unbroken stretch for a solid line, both lines of a double line; the head of an arrow). "partial" if you can name the marking but a deciding feature is hidden. "insufficient" if no usable part of a marking is visible.
- marking.marking_type: one of the listed values. white_* and yellow_* are longitudinal lines; yellow_solid_dashed is a solid and a broken yellow line side by side; two_way_left_turn_lane is a centre lane whose two edges are each a solid and a broken yellow line, usually with pairs of white arrows pointing opposite ways; arrow is a lane-use arrow; other is any other marking (crosswalk, stop line, text ...); none_visible means no marking can be seen.
- marking.arrow_type: the arrow's directions, only for arrows; otherwise null.
- marking.position: where the marking is relative to my car: left, right, or in_ego_lane (inside my lane, such as an arrow I am driving over, or a centre turn lane my car is in).
- actions: what THIS marking means for my car. The six actions:
  - cross_line: crossing this line from my lane (changing lanes or overtaking across it).
  - go_straight, turn_left, turn_right, u_turn: movements out of the lane the marking belongs to: at the next intersection for a lane-use arrow; for a two-way left-turn lane, turn_left also means turning left out of the centre lane between intersections (into a side road or driveway).
  - enter_center_lane_to_wait: entering a centre lane to wait for a left turn.
  Which actions a marking governs: a longitudinal line governs only cross_line; a lane-use arrow governs only go_straight, turn_left, turn_right and u_turn; a two-way left-turn lane governs only enter_center_lane_to_wait and turn_left; any other marking (crosswalk, stop line, text) governs none of the six. Every action the marking does not govern is "not_applicable".
  For each governed action: "allowed" = the marking does not forbid it (an action that is only discouraged counts as allowed); "prohibited" = the marking forbids it; "not_determined" = the photo hides the feature that decides it (only with partial or insufficient evidence). With full evidence, every governed action is either allowed or prohibited.
